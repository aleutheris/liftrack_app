## Statement

The system shall make every gym-time interaction — reading the next prescribed exercise and
logging one completed set — operable with one thumb, without keyboard entry, and legible at
arm's length, on a 360 × 640 CSS-px viewport.

## Rationale

The user is standing at a rack between sets: one hand may be on the bar, the free hand holds
the phone, fingers are sweaty or chalky, and the screen is read at arm's length under gym
lighting. Each number below is stated with the reasoning that produced it, so a reviewer can
disagree with the reasoning rather than with taste.

## Acceptance Criteria (behavioral)

- Every control reachable during a logged workout has a hit area of at least 48 × 48 CSS px,
  with at least 8 CSS px of clear space between adjacent hit areas, verified by a DOM
  measurement over the workout screen at 360 × 640. (48 px ≈ 8–9 mm on a typical phone, about
  the pad width of an adult index finger, and a wet fingertip lands wider still; a reviewer
  preferring 44 px — Apple's figure — or 56 px should amend the number here.)
- Logging one completed set costs at most 2 deliberate touches when reps and weight match the
  prescription or the previous set, and at most 6 when either value needs adjusting, with no
  on-screen keyboard required for the common case. Verified by a touch-count assertion; the
  control widget (stepper, slider, or otherwise) is an implementation choice this requirement
  does not prescribe. (18 logs in a 6-exercise 3-set session ⇒ a 108-touch ceiling.)
- Every control counted above renders within the lower 50% of the viewport at 360 × 640, so it
  is reachable by the thumb of the hand holding the phone without a regrip.
- Reps, weight and set index render at ≥ 24 CSS px and ≥ 7:1 contrast; all other workout-screen
  text at ≥ 16 CSS px and ≥ 4.5:1. (24 CSS px ≈ 4 mm of glyph height, ~25 arcminutes at 60 cm,
  against the ~16 a glance needs; 7:1 rather than 4.5:1 because of glare and a smeared screen.)
- No gym-time interaction depends on hover, drag, swipe, long-press or multi-touch; every
  action completes on a single tap, verified by an end-to-end run emitting tap events only.
