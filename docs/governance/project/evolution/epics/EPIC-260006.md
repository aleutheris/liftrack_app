- Epic ID: EPIC-260006
- Title: Raise Liftrack's read-pattern needs into the Crystord contract
- Persona/Actor: Liftrack as a named consumer of the Crystord contract, represented to the backend
  owner by the repository owner.
- Value statement: For Liftrack, turn the read limits it cannot work around into recorded,
  evidence-backed requests against the Crystord contract, rather than absorbing them forever.
- Context: The backend is a moving target. Its ICR-260024 is pending, not approved, and would make
  `retrieve`'s bounds client-reachable and add nullable atom timestamps with range filters; its
  EPIC-260101 records that pagination is implemented, tested and unreachable; its EPIC-260099
  carries a deferred limit raise. Liftrack appears in none of those records' consumer tables.
- Problem statement: `retrieve` caps at 25 with no signal and atoms carry no timestamps. Each is
  only partially workable around, and a silently truncated read is a correctness defect in Liftrack
  that Liftrack cannot fix in its code.
- Expected outcome: ICR-260001 and ICR-260003 are in front of the backend owner with their
  evidence, and Liftrack has a recorded position on what it does while they are undecided.
- Success metric(s): both ICRs leave Open with a recorded decision; Liftrack is named in the
  backend's consumer-impact table before that record's decision gate is settled; every workaround
  Liftrack ships is recorded together with the approval that deletes it.
- Non-goals: implementing anything inside the engine; re-proposing what the backend already
  proposes; requesting capability Liftrack has decided to absorb; gating a release on an approval.
- Constraints: Liftrack has no authority to change an interface and none of these requests may
  assume approval; each must carry its own compatibility statement; none may assume a fold-in to
  another project's pending record.
- Dependencies: the backend owner's attention and decision; the read patterns EPIC-260001 ratifies,
  which are the evidence every one of these requests rests on.
- Risks/assumptions: assumes the backend's pending record lands roughly as drafted, which is not
  approved and may be amended or rejected; assumes the 25/100 bounds are applied rather than raised,
  so the ceiling moves to 100 instead of vanishing; assumes filing indiscriminately costs credibility.
- Target window: TBD.
- Validation approach: each ICR carries its own verification plan with a red state provable today —
  26 atoms under one label returning 25 with no signal — paired with a positive control; Liftrack's
  own check is that every workaround it ships names the record whose approval removes it.

## Ideation Bullets

- [ ] **In what order are the two filed?** The documentation half of ICR-260001 costs one
  paragraph in the backend user guide, no schema text and no version bump, and closes the largest
  silent-data-loss surface for every consumer the engine has. Filing it first tests the channel
  cheaply and does not touch anyone else's decision gate.
- [ ] **Does Liftrack ask to be folded into the backend's pending record, or keep its own?** A
  fold-in saves a second version bump and a second round of client pin moves; the engine's own
  precedent is to split records precisely so one clean approval gate is not muddied by another, and
  that record is already at its decision point. Offering a fold-in is not the same as assuming it.
- [ ] **What does Liftrack do if that record is rejected or stalls indefinitely?** Ship the
  workarounds — a month-bucket key, a category-paginated catalog read, roll-up atoms — or wait.
  Shipping means writing history that a later approval does not un-write, so the workaround has to
  be thin, and the one designated as the removable seam has to be named before anything is written.
- [ ] **Is the truncation-signal or count ask worth filing now?** Once page bounds are reachable, a
  client can overfetch by one and infer that more exist, which may make a count unnecessary; a count
  also roughly doubles the read cost of a view that renders rows anyway. Filing early risks asking
  for something a cheap client trick replaces.
- [ ] **Which asks does Liftrack deliberately not make?** An append or partial-update mutation,
  full-text search, deletion tombstones, a change to the label-listing cap, and a sort-direction
  argument are all absorbed on purpose. Recording the abstentions is what stops them resurfacing
  later as if they had been open all along.
- [ ] **What is Liftrack's response to the next minor schema release?** Two sibling consumers hard
  block on a purely additive minor because of how they pinned. Liftrack must choose its pin before
  it ships and decide whether an additively-newer backend warns or blocks — and whether it asks to
  be added to the consumer table before that release is decided.
- [ ] **Are the non-read observations raised at all?** The bond vocabulary the published guide
  contradicts, `signin` declared a Query rather than a Mutation, and the typed-content fields the
  design depends on but the guide never defines are each cheap, and none is on the critical path.
